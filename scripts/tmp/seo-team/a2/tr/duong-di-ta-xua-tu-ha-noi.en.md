**The road to Tà Xùa from Hanoi** is about 210km long and follows four main routes: National Road 32 (QL32) through Sơn Tây and Thanh Sơn; National Road 32B (QL32B) over Cón Pass into Sơn La; National Road 37 (QL37) through Phù Yên to Bắc Yên; and finally 14km of provincial road 112 (ĐT112) climbing the mountain. By car it takes about 6 hours of continuous driving, plus 1 to 1.5 hours if you stop to eat and rest. Most of the way is easy national road; the hard sections are concentrated on Cón Pass and the last 14km.

**Quick summary:**

- **Route:** Hanoi, Sơn Tây, Trung Hà Bridge, Thanh Sơn, Thu Cúc junction, Cón Pass, Mường Cơi, Phù Yên, Bắc Yên, Tà Xùa.
- **Distance measured on the map:** 207 to 210km from Hanoi's Old Quarter to the centre of Tà Xùa. Some apps show up to 245km because they choose an exit on the north side of the city.
- **Two mountain passes:** Cón Pass on National Road 32B between Phú Thọ and Sơn La, and the 14km provincial road 112 from Bắc Yên up to the centre of Tà Xùa.
- **Tạ Khoa Bridge:** not on the road from Hanoi via National Road 32. You only cross this bridge when taking the Mộc Châu route.
- **Season to be careful:** June to September brings heavy rain; provincial road 112 had a major landslide in August 2024.
- **Don't want to drive yourself:** overnight cabin buses leave Hanoi at 9pm to 10pm and reach Tà Xùa around 6am.

### Stage 1: Hanoi to Sơn Tây, across Trung Hà Bridge to Thanh Sơn

The first 90km or so cross the delta and midlands. From the inner city, head towards Sơn Tây, then cross the Đà River at Trung Hà Bridge into Phú Thọ and join National Road 32 to Thanh Sơn.

- **Road surface:** asphalt, two lanes, passing many towns and markets. Average speed is lower than on an expressway because of the dense population on both sides.
- **Best time to pass:** early morning. The western gateway of Hanoi is often congested at rush hour, on Friday afternoons and on Sunday afternoons.
- **Rest stop:** Thanh Sơn is the usual place for breakfast and the first fuel stop.

## Stage 2: Thu Cúc, Cón Pass on National Road 32B to Mường Cơi

At Thu Cúc junction (former Tân Sơn), National Road 32 continues towards former Yên Bái; you turn onto **National Road 32B** to enter Sơn La. This is where many guides lump it together as "turn onto National Road 37"; in reality you only meet National Road 37 at Mường Cơi junction.

National Road 32B includes **Cón Pass**, the mountain road linking Tân Sơn (Phú Thọ) with Phù Yên (Sơn La). Dân Việt describes the pass as long, winding and treacherous. The stretch from Thu Cúc to Mường Cơi is only about 19km, but it is the first time the vehicle has to climb and descend continuously.

Tips for this stretch:

- Slow down before every blind bend and give a short honk.
- Do not overtake trucks on uphill sections.
- People prone to motion sickness should sit in the front and look far ahead along the road. More tips are in the article [motion sickness on the passes up to Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/meo-chong-say-xe-deo-ta-xua).

## Stage 3: National Road 37 through Phù Yên to Bắc Yên

At Mường Cơi junction, you join **National Road 37** heading west. This section passes through former Phù Yên town, which has petrol stations and eateries, a good place for a lunch break.

From Phù Yên to Bắc Yên is about 30km. Following the order of places Wikipedia lists on National Road 37, the road passes Gia Phù and Suối Tọ before reaching Bắc Yên. It is a paved national road, with some sections curving along hillsides; a few spots have had rockfalls after rain, such as the rockfall at km386 through Tường Phù commune (Phù Yên) reported in the press.

Bắc Yên is the former town area in the valley at the foot of Tà Xùa mountain, now part of Bắc Yên commune. It is the last place to:

- Fill up your tank before going up the mountain.
- Withdraw cash; the actual situation with banking, phone signal and wifi on the mountain is covered in the article [Does Tà Xùa have ATMs, wifi and phone signal](/kinh-nghiem/song-dien-thoai-atm-wifi-ta-xua).
- Change vehicles if travelling by cabin bus: sleeper buses usually reach Bắc Yên around 4am, and passengers switch to the operator's smaller vehicle to go up the mountain. Which operators drop you right at your homestay door is compared in the article [do cabin buses drop off at the homestay](/kinh-nghiem/xe-cabin-tra-khach-tan-homestay-ta-xua).

## Is the 14km of provincial road 112 from Bắc Yên up to Tà Xùa difficult?

This is the section people talk about most. **Provincial road 112** runs from Bắc Yên up to the centre of Tà Xùa and then on to Xím Vàng and Hang Chú, more than 50km in total. The part from Bắc Yên to the centre is about 14km long: Wikipedia says Tà Xùa commune is 14.5km from Bắc Yên commune, and the two map services measured 13.7 to 13.8km.

Characteristics according to Báo Sơn La and VnExpress:

- **Steep, with many sharp bends one after another**, some stretches only wide enough for one car, where you must stop to let oncoming vehicles pass.
- **A deep drop on one side** in many places; signage is incomplete.
- **Narrow road not yet widened:** in November 2025 the communes were still petitioning the province to upgrade the Bắc Yên to Tà Xùa section soon.

Maps estimate about 30 minutes by car when the road is empty, but in practice it usually takes longer. Use a low gear going uphill and engine braking going downhill; do not ride the brakes the whole way. Driving details for cars (low-clearance cars, automatic gearboxes, parking) are in the article [driving yourself to Tà Xùa by car](/kinh-nghiem/di-ta-xua-bang-o-to-tu-lai).

Once at the centre, provincial road 112 continues past Đỉnh Gió and Làng Chếu to Xím Vàng. In the ripe rice season you can add 19km to Mâm Xôi hill; see the article [Where is Xím Vàng, the road to Trạm Mâm Xôi](/kinh-nghiem/xim-vang-mua-lua-chin).

## Where is Tạ Khoa Bridge, and does the Mộc Châu route cross it?

Many people think the road to Tà Xùa must cross Tạ Khoa Bridge over the Đà River. Via National Road 32 it does not: the route from Hanoi through Thu Cúc, Mường Cơi and Phù Yên does not cross the Đà River in the Bắc Yên area. Tạ Khoa Bridge is on National Road 37 **west of Bắc Yên**, towards Yên Châu and Mai Sơn.

About the bridge: Wikipedia and Hà Nội Mới newspaper record that it opened to traffic in August 2003 as the first permanent concrete bridge across the Đà River, more than 550m long, replacing the earlier Tạ Khoa ferry.

You will cross this bridge when combining Tà Xùa with Mộc Châu:

| Route | Roads | Distance | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mộc Châu, Cò Nòi, Tạ Khoa Bridge, Bắc Yên | National Road 6, National Road 37, provincial road 112 | about 135km to the centre of Tà Xùa (measured on the map) | No ferry |
| Mộc Châu, Vạn Yên ferry, Gia Phù, Bắc Yên | National Road 43, National Road 37, provincial road 112 | shorter, depends on ferry times | National Road 43 is 105km long, ferry at km27; Vạn Yên Bridge not yet built |

An itinerary combining the two places is in the article [from Mộc Châu, Sơn La to Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/tu-moc-chau-son-la-sang-ta-xua). Travellers coming from Lào Cai or Sa Pa can see the article [from Lào Cai, Sa Pa to Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/tu-lao-cai-di-ta-xua).

| Vehicle | Driving time | Total time to allow | Suitable for |

By car, reach the centre of Tà Xùa while it is still light. If you are torn between driving yourself and taking a bus, see the article [from Hanoi to Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/tu-ha-noi-di-ta-xua), which compares 5 cabin bus operators and pick-up points.

## Rainy season and fog: when should you change your plans?

June to September is the rainy season in the mountains of Sơn La. Two incidents on provincial road 112 show why you need to keep an eye on the weather:

- **In 2022:** Báo Sơn La recorded 5 landslide points with about 1,500m³ of earth and rock from km9 to km13.
- **On 10/8/2024:** about 50,000m³ of earth and rock slid down at km13+600 on the section through Tà Xùa village, burying a homestay, killing 1 person and injuring 2; the road was cut and 12 households had to be evacuated (Znews, VOH).

What to do in practice:

- If heavy rain lasts a day or two before your trip: call your homestay to ask about road conditions and be ready to postpone.
- Fog often comes down from late afternoon, especially in winter. In fog, switch on low beams, slow down and keep to the right edge of the road.
- Read more in the articles [is travelling to Tà Xùa dangerous](/kinh-nghiem/di-ta-xua-co-nguy-hiem-khong) and [Tà Xùa in summer, May to August](/kinh-nghiem/ta-xua-mua-he-thang-5-8).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which national roads does the route from Hanoi to Tà Xùa follow?

Take National Road 32 through Sơn Tây, Trung Hà Bridge and Thanh Sơn to Thu Cúc junction, turn onto National Road 32B over Cón Pass to Mường Cơi, follow National Road 37 through Phù Yên to Bắc Yên, then climb about 14km of provincial road 112 to the centre of Tà Xùa.

### How many km is the road to Tà Xùa?

From Hanoi's Old Quarter to the centre of Tà Xùa is about 207 to 210km via National Road 32, measured with two map services. Newspapers give 200 to 240km depending on the starting point.

### How long is the pass from Bắc Yên up to Tà Xùa?

About 14km along provincial road 112. The road is steep and narrow with many sharp bends, and some stretches are only wide enough for one car; as of November 2025 this section had still not been upgraded and widened.

### Does the trip to Tà Xùa cross Tạ Khoa Bridge?

No, if you go from Hanoi via National Road 32. Tạ Khoa Bridge is on National Road 37 west of Bắc Yên; you only cross the Đà River on this bridge when taking the Mộc Châu route via Cò Nòi.

### How long does it take to ride a motorbike from Hanoi to Tà Xùa?

About 6 to 7 hours of riding, almost a full day including breaks. Leave at first light so you climb provincial road 112 while it is still light.

### Which season is the road to Tà Xùa easiest?

October to April has little rain, dry roads and a low risk of landslides; on the other hand, early mornings and late afternoons are often foggy. From June to September, check for heavy rain before you go.

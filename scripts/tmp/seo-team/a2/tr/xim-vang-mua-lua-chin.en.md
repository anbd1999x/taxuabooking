**Where is Xím Vàng?** Xím Vàng is a highland commune right on the western edge of Tà Xùa commune (Sơn La), known for its rice terraces and the Mâm Xôi hill. From central Tà Xùa, follow provincial road 112 past Đỉnh Gió and Làng Chếu for about 19km, roughly 40 minutes by motorbike. The rice here ripens from mid-September to October, and it usually looks its best in late September and early October.

**Quick summary:**

- **Location:** Xím Vàng commune, Sơn La province, bordering Tà Xùa commune to the west. Since mid-2025, the new Xím Vàng commune includes the former Xím Vàng and Hang Chú.
- **Distance:** about 19km from central Tà Xùa, 17km from Đỉnh Gió and 15km from Làng Chếu to Trạm Mâm Xôi, all along provincial road 112.
- **Main viewpoint:** the Mâm Xôi hill in Xím Vàng village, where the paddy banks form circles around a single hill. Trạm Mâm Xôi is a wooden hut with a coffee counter overlooking the hill.
- **Rice season:** planted from May to June, one crop a year; ripe from mid-September to October.
- **Cloud hunting:** stay in Tà Xùa, hunt clouds in the morning, and only head to Xím Vàng in the afternoon to see the rice.
- **Getting there:** rent a motorbike from 180.000đ a day, or hire a local taxi for the whole day at 1.700.000đ to 2.000.000đ per car.

## Where is Xím Vàng and which commune is it in?

Xím Vàng lies on the western slope of the Tà Xùa massif, in the same province of Sơn La. Before the district level was abolished, both Xím Vàng and Tà Xùa belonged to Bắc Yên district. Under the administrative reorganization effective from 16/6/2025, Hang Chú commune was merged into Xím Vàng, and the new commune kept the name Xím Vàng.

A few figures to give you a picture:

- **Altitude:** the rice-growing villages sit around 1.500m, about the same as central Tà Xùa.
- **Residents:** mostly H'Mông people. The rice terraces were carved into the mountainsides by the locals themselves over many generations.
- **Paddy area:** according to Sơn La Newspaper in 2021, the former Xím Vàng commune had 321 ha of rice terraces, most of them in the villages of Sồng Chống, Xím Vàng, Háng Chơ and Háng Gò Bua.
- **Compared with Bắc Yên:** the commune center is more than 30km from the former Bắc Yên town, meaning you have to climb the whole pass up to Tà Xùa and then keep riding west.

When searching on a map, type "Trạm Mâm Xôi Xím Vàng" or "Xím Vàng, Sơn La". Mù Cang Chải (former Yên Bái) also has a very famous Mâm Xôi hill; that is a different place, about a hundred kilometers of mountain road away.

## How far is Xím Vàng from central Tà Xùa, and which road do you take?

There is only one main route: **provincial road 112**. This road runs from Bắc Yên up to central Tà Xùa, past Đỉnh Gió and Làng Chếu, then down into Xím Vàng and Hang Chú. Sơn La Newspaper puts the whole Bắc Yên to Hang Chú route at more than 50km.

Trạm Mâm Xôi is right by the road; park on the shoulder and walk into the hut.

## What is Trạm Mâm Xôi, and where do you see the Mâm Xôi hill?

The Mâm Xôi hill is in Xím Vàng village: a round hill in the middle of the valley, with paddies built up in concentric rings of banks from the foot to the top, which from above looks like a tray of sticky rice. On the summit there is a small wooden hut and a few flags.

**Trạm Mâm Xôi** is the name you will see on maps and on the wooden sign by the road. It is a wooden hut built on the edge of the slope above the hill, its roof hung with drying corn cobs, and a sign reading "trạm mâm xôi cà phê". The wooden deck juts out over the valley, and from here you can see the whole Mâm Xôi hill below.

![The wooden Trạm Mâm Xôi hut in Xím Vàng with its trạm mâm xôi cà phê sign, drying corn cobs hanging from the roof, a wooden deck facing the rice terraces](/uploads/1794462912490-55dc3ecd6669.jpg)

Three viewpoints visitors often choose:

1. **The wooden deck of Trạm Mâm Xôi:** looks straight down at the round hill, good for panoramic shots.
2. **The roadside above the valley:** shows many terraces stretching along the mountainside, including the roofs of the village.
3. **The footpath down to the foot of the hill:** steep, and the soil is slippery when there is mist. Only go down if your shoes grip well, and stay on the paddy banks the locals leave as paths.

If a hut or a paddy has a sign asking for a photo fee, pay as the sign says: it is someone's field, and they spend the whole year building up those banks.

## Which month does the rice ripen in Xím Vàng?

Xím Vàng has only **one rice crop** a year. Locals plant from late May into June, the rice ripens from mid-September and is gradually harvested through October. Sơn La Newspaper, Công an Nhân dân and VnExpress all name late September to early October as the best window.

| Time | What the paddies look like | Worth visiting? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May to June | Paddies are flooded and seedlings planted; the banks stand out clearly | Yes, if you like photos of flooded terraces |
| July to early September | Green rice, rainy season, roads easily slippery | Fine when the weather is dry |
| Mid-September to early October | The rice turns golden, then evenly golden | The best time to go |
| Mid to late October | Paddies are harvested bit by bit, golden plots mixed with empty ones | Go early in the month |
| November to April | Fields rest, only stubble left | Not worth going just for the fields |

Rice at altitude does not ripen all at once: lower, sunnier plots turn golden first, higher plots a few days later. In rainy years, the ripening season can shift by one to two weeks. To be sure, message your homestay and ask for photos of the fields close to your travel date.

September also coincides with the 2/9 holiday, when rooms in Tà Xùa are charged at holiday rates. See prices and booking tips in the article [Tà Xùa in September](/kinh-nghiem/ta-xua-thang-9); for October, when the clouds start to return, read [Tà Xùa in October](/kinh-nghiem/ta-xua-thang-10).

## Can you hunt clouds in Xím Vàng?

You may see mist and low clouds in the Xím Vàng valley early in the morning, but few people stay overnight here; most visitors stay in Tà Xùa. The popular combination is **cloud hunting in Tà Xùa in the morning, Xím Vàng in the afternoon**:

- **5:30 to 8:00:** watch the clouds at Đỉnh Gió or from your homestay balcony. Đỉnh Gió is right on the road to Xím Vàng; see the article [Đỉnh Gió Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/dinh-gio-ta-xua).
- **8:00 to 13:00:** breakfast, rest, check out if needed.
- **14:00:** ride over to Xím Vàng. The slanting afternoon light makes the paddy banks stand out and the rice shows its color better than at midday.
- **17:00:** head back to Tà Xùa, arriving before dark and before the mist covers the pass.

The ripe rice season is the start of the cloud season, so there are not clouds every day. How to predict clouds from the afternoon before is covered in the article [cloud hunting tips in Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/kinh-nghiem-san-may-ta-xua).

If you want to stay closer to Xím Vàng, choose a homestay in Làng Chếu: [La Chếu Homestay](/homestay/la-cheu-homestay) has 20 rooms with weekday list prices from 675.000đ; [Khèn Coffee House](/homestay/khen-coffee-house-ta-xua) has cloud-view rooms from 700.000đ on weekdays. From here to Trạm Mâm Xôi is only about 15km.

## What should you keep in mind when visiting Xím Vàng?

- **Do not step into the paddies.** Rice close to harvest falls over very easily. Take photos from the banks or the roadside, and do not lead large groups into the middle of a plot.
- **Ask before photographing people.** Locals are harvesting and carrying rice; ask first and accept it if they say no. Read more in the article [responsible travel in Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/du-lich-ta-xua-van-minh).
- **Drones:** the valley is wide and good for flying, but avoid flying low over people working in the fields. Rules and flying spots are in the article [flying a drone in Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/flycam-ta-xua).
- **Rainy season roads:** in August 2024, a major landslide on provincial road 112 near Tà Xùa village cut the road. After heavy rain, ask your homestay about road conditions before setting off.
- **What to bring:** a light jacket because mountain afternoons cool down fast, drinking water, small cash, and shoes with good grip if you plan to go down to the foot of the hill.
- **Rented motorbikes:** check the brakes and fuel before leaving the center; the round trip is about 40km of steep road.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is Xím Vàng?

Xím Vàng is a highland commune of Sơn La province, right on the western edge of Tà Xùa commune. From central Tà Xùa, follow provincial road 112 past Đỉnh Gió and Làng Chếu for about 19km to reach Trạm Mâm Xôi.

### How long does it take to get from Tà Xùa to Xím Vàng?

About 40 minutes by motorbike from central Tà Xùa, or 30 minutes if you start from Làng Chếu. Including photo stops, set aside at least an afternoon for the round trip.

### What is Trạm Mâm Xôi in Xím Vàng?

Trạm Mâm Xôi is a wooden hut with a coffee counter built on the edge of the slope above the Mâm Xôi hill in Xím Vàng village. From the hut's wooden deck you look straight down at the rings of rice terraces around the hill.

### Which month does the rice in Xím Vàng ripen?

The rice ripens from mid-September to October, usually looking best in late September and early October. There is only one crop a year; after the harvest the fields rest until planting season in May.

### Are there homestays in Xím Vàng, and where should you stay?

Most visitors stay in Tà Xùa and go as a day trip. To be as close to Xím Vàng as possible, choose a homestay in Làng Chếu such as La Chếu or Khèn Coffee House, about 15km from Trạm Mâm Xôi.

### Is the road to Xím Vàng difficult?

The road is narrow, with many slopes and sharp bends, especially the stretch up to Đỉnh Gió. Riders not used to mountain passes should take a local taxi or ride pillion with someone who knows the road.

### Are Xím Vàng and the Mâm Xôi hill in Mù Cang Chải the same place?

No. The Mâm Xôi hill in Xím Vàng is in Sơn La, next to Tà Xùa. The Mâm Xôi hill in Mù Cang Chải is in the former Yên Bái area, a completely different spot.

Most visitors just ride into Háng Đồng to photograph the Dinosaur Spine and ride back out. A pity — because **Háng Đồng village** is where Tà Xùa's original character is best preserved: a H'Mông village nestled in a valley of clouds, 12–14 km from the centre.

## What's in Háng Đồng village?

- **Traditional wooden houses** of the H'Mông scattered over the hillside, with morning clouds weaving between the roofs.
- **Small rice terraces** following the ravines — beautiful both in the flooding season (May–June) and at harvest (September).
- **A slow pace of life**: children playing in the yards, adults roasting tea and weaving cloth. An afternoon wandering the village gives you more than any check-in spot.

## Stay a night

Sleeping at a homestay around Háng Đồng has two advantages: the next morning the [Dinosaur Spine](/kinh-nghiem/song-lung-khung-long-ta-xua) is just minutes away (instead of getting up at 4:30 to ride from the centre), and nights in the village are utterly quiet, with the Milky Way visible on clear nights. Filter homestays by area on the [homestay page](/homestay). Before visiting the village, read our guide to [Hmong culture in Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/van-hoa-nguoi-hmong-ta-xua) on the Hmong New Year, dress and etiquette.

## Things to note when visiting the village

- Greet people and ask permission before photographing them, especially children and the elderly.
- Don't give money/sweets to children — if you want to help, buy the village's produce.
- The road in is very steep: less confident riders should take a [local motorbike taxi](/xe-om).
- Bring food and water; there are few shops in the village.

Combine: Dinosaur Spine in the morning → village lunch → afternoon wandering Háng Đồng → evening stargazing back at the homestay. A whole day without your smartphone and you won't miss it.

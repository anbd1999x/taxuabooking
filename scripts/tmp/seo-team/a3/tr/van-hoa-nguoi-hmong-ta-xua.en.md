The Hmong (H'Mông) people (written as "Mông" in official documents) are the main residents of Tà Xùa: the villages around the cloud-hunting area are all Hmong villages, and in the former Bắc Yên district the Hmong make up more than 45% of the population. Hmong New Year comes about a month before Lunar New Year (Tết Nguyên đán); this season it falls at the start of the 12th lunar month, around 08/01/2027. This article gathers what visitors should know to see village culture the right way: the New Year, clothing, homes, the khèn pipes and how to behave in the villages.

**Quick summary:**

- **Today's Tà Xùa commune** combines three former communes, Tà Xùa, Háng Đồng and Làng Chếu, merged on 01/07/2025. Háng Đồng village, home of the Dinosaur Spine ridge, lies within this commune.
- **Hmong New Year** begins at the start of the 12th lunar month. The 1st day of the 12th month in the year of Bính Ngọ is Friday 08/01/2027.
- **The New Year tray** must have bánh dày, sticky rice cakes pounded from upland glutinous rice; the first 12 cakes, one for each month, are offered to heaven, earth and the harvest spirits.
- **Clothing** is colourful brocade; local people have revived indigo dyeing and beeswax drawing as a hands-on experience from about 300.000đ per person.
- **Easiest village to visit:** Thôn Bụa A on the road to the Grassland, prettiest from 7am to 10am.
- **Rule number 1:** ask permission before photographing local people, especially children and the elderly.

## Which villages do the Hmong live in around Tà Xùa?

Tà Xùa is a highland commune of Sơn La province, with its centre at about 1.500m above sea level. Village life is mostly upland farming: corn, upland rice, tea, Hmong mustard greens and black chickens raised free on the hills.

From 01/07/2025, under Nghị quyết 1681/NQ-UBTVQH15, the new Tà Xùa commune was formed from three former communes:

- **Former Tà Xùa:** the central area, with most of the homestays, eateries and Đỉnh Gió (Wind Peak).
- **Former Háng Đồng:** includes Chống Tra village with the Dinosaur Spine, the Dolphin Rock (Mỏm cá heo) and the Grassland.
- **Former Làng Chếu:** the Khèn Coffee House area, where people watch the cloud waterfall.

The neighbouring commune is Xím Vàng, which now also includes the former Hang Chú commune, famous for the Mâm Xôi hill rice terraces. So in older articles you will see names like "Háng Đồng commune", "Làng Chếu commune" and "Hang Chú commune". These are pre-2025 administrative names, not different places. The general location is covered in [Where is Tà Xùa and how far is it from Hanoi](/kinh-nghiem/ta-xua-o-dau-cach-ha-noi-bao-nhieu-km).

## When is Hmong New Year in Tà Xùa, and what happens?

The Hmong New Year begins at the start of the 12th lunar month, about a month before Lunar New Year. By the end of the 11th lunar month, when the village peach trees bloom, families are already preparing. Each family picks its own day for the rituals depending on its circumstances, so there is no single date for the whole village.

| 2026–2027 season milestone | Solar calendar date | Notes for visitors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End of the 11th lunar month | late 12/2026 | Families prepare, pound cakes, butcher meat |
| 1st day of the 12th lunar month | Friday 08/01/2027 | Hmong New Year in full swing |
| Lunar New Year | 06/02/2027 | Tết room rates on Tà Xùa Booking start from this night |

Rituals and customs recorded in Hmong villages of the Bắc Yên area:

- **Driving away the bad things of the old year** with a ceremony at home.
- **Pasting paper seals on farm tools.** Ploughs, harrows, knives and hammers get paper pasted on them and are set beside the altar, as thanks to the tools that helped on the fields all year.
- **Bánh dày.** Upland sticky rice is steamed, pounded by young men with wooden pestles, shaped into rounds and wrapped in banana leaves. The round cakes symbolise the sun and the moon. The first six pairs, that is 12 cakes for 12 months, are offered to heaven, earth and the harvest spirits.
- **Games and khèn music.** Young people play pao ball tossing, sing call-and-response songs and play the khèn in the first days of the year.

Visiting Tà Xùa at this time has an advantage: Lunar New Year prices have not kicked in yet, and the night of 08/01/2027 is an ordinary Friday night. You also get thick cloud season and white tớ dày cherry blossoms; the week-by-week calendar is in [Tà Xùa in January](/kinh-nghiem/ta-xua-thang-1). Rituals are a private family matter, so visitors should only watch when invited.

## What is special about Hmong brocade clothing and indigo dyeing?

On ordinary days, older people and children in the villages still wear brocade tops and skirts. The New Year is when many people put on new clothes in the brightest colours.

Two techniques create Hmong cloth:

- **Beeswax drawing.** The maker uses hot beeswax to draw patterns on white cloth. Areas covered in wax will not take the dye.
- **Indigo dyeing.** The cloth is dipped in indigo many times to reach a deep blue, then boiled to remove the wax. White patterns appear on the indigo background.

This craft had almost died out in Tà Xùa, but local people have revived it as a visitor experience. At Nhuộm Chàm Tà Xùa, you draw with wax, dip the cloth in indigo and sew a small scarf or bag yourself. Each activity takes about 2 to 5 hours and costs from about 300.000đ per person. Each session takes a limited number of guests, so book ahead and ask for the exact workshop location. Wear dark clothes, because indigo stains your hands for a long time. ## Wooden houses, earthen walls: what do village homes look like?

On provincial road 112 or the road to the Grassland, you will see low-roofed wooden houses scattered across the hillsides, surrounded by corn fields, tea gardens, pear trees and sơn tra (Hmong apple) trees. In the early morning mist drifts over the roofs, which is the shot many people come to the villages for.

A few points to read Hmong homes correctly:

- **Rammed-earth houses** (thick packed-earth walls) are a style tied to the Hmong of the Hà Giang stone plateau. In Tà Xùa, wooden houses are more common in the villages.
- **Earthen walls in homestays.** Some places to stay build old-style earthen walls. [Phỏng Lài Homestay](/homestay/phong-lai-homstay) has earthen walls, arched doors and 11 rooms, all with bathtubs, at 650.000đ per night.
- **Porches and kitchens are private family spaces.** Do not step into a yard or kitchen without being invited.

To see a village at rest, stay a night in an area far from the centre such as Háng Đồng. The article [Háng Đồng village](/kinh-nghiem/ban-hang-dong-ta-xua) describes the pace of life there.

## Khèn, ancient tea, black chicken: what else is tied to the Hmong?

**Musical instruments.** The Hmong have the khèn, the jaw harp, flutes and the two-string fiddle. The khèn is most familiar at New Year, when young men play and dance at the same time. On ordinary days a khèn performance is hard to find, so do not book it with a homestay as a guaranteed show.

**Ancient Shan tuyết tea.** In the Bắc Yên area, ancient tea trees grow only around Tà Xùa. Bẹ village is said to have the most old tea trees in the commune. The Hmong pick tea by hand and roast it in cast-iron pans. How to choose tea and prices are in [Tà Xùa Shan tuyết tea](/kinh-nghiem/tra-shan-tuyet-ta-xua).

**Food.** Black chicken is a native Hmong breed, raised free on the hills, so the meat is firm. Hmong mustard greens grow on high fields, crunchy and slightly bitter. Sơn tra fruit and sơn tra wine belong to autumn. Menus and prices are in [What to eat in Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/an-gi-o-ta-xua).

**Wooden-house café.** [Nhà H'Mông](/cafe/nha-h-mong) is a wooden-house café in the centre, open from 6:30am to 11pm, good for cold evenings when many other places have closed.

## How should you behave when visiting villages and photographing Hmong people?

Tà Xùa welcomes more visitors every year, and the villages are where people live, not a film set. Here are eight rules we recommend:

1. **Ask before taking photos.** A greeting and a questioning gesture are enough. If someone shakes their head, put the camera away.
2. **Do not give money or sweets to children.** It creates a habit of begging. If you want to help, buy farm produce and brocade from the village.
3. **Do not enter houses, gardens or fields uninvited.** Ask first, even if you only want to stand in a yard to shoot the mountains.
4. **Do not enter a house during a ritual**, unless invited. Be extra considerate during Hmong New Year.
5. **Ride slowly through villages,** do not rev your engine. Give way to people on foot and to livestock.
6. **Do not break off peach or plum branches** for photos. The trees in the gardens belong to each family.
7. **Bargain lightly** when buying vegetables, tea or sơn tra. A few thousand dong is small for us but a whole market morning for locals.
8. **Take your rubbish down the mountain.** The grassy hills and trails have no bins.

The full set of rules for both nature and villages is in [Responsible travel in Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/du-lich-ta-xua-van-minh).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which ethnic group lives in Tà Xùa?

The main residents of Tà Xùa are the Mông people, also called Hmong (H'Mông). The villages around the cloud-hunting area, such as Háng Đồng, Chống Tra and Bụa A, are all Hmong villages.

### When is Hmong New Year in Tà Xùa?

Hmong New Year begins at the start of the 12th lunar month, about a month before Lunar New Year. In the 2026–2027 season, the 1st day of the 12th lunar month is Friday 08/01/2027.

### Can visitors watch Hmong New Year?

Visitors can still enjoy the New Year atmosphere in the villages, such as pao ball tossing and khèn music. Rituals are a private family matter, and you should only watch when the host invites you.

### Is there an indigo dyeing experience in Tà Xùa?

Yes. Nhuộm Chàm Tà Xùa lets visitors draw with beeswax, dip cloth in indigo and sew a scarf, taking about 2 to 5 hours, from about 300.000đ per person. Booking ahead is recommended.

### Is it OK to photograph Hmong children in Tà Xùa?

Only after asking permission and with an adult's consent. Do not give money or sweets in exchange for a photo.

### Which commune do Hang Chú, Háng Đồng and Làng Chếu belong to now?

From 01/07/2025, Háng Đồng and Làng Chếu merged into Tà Xùa commune; Hang Chú merged into Xím Vàng commune. Both communes are in Sơn La province.

Trekking Sa Mu is a real mountain route right next to Tà Xùa, and it is not the same as Tà Xùa Peak at 2.865m. Sa Mu Peak, which climbers also call U Bò, stands 2.756m high according to most sources. It sits inside the Tà Xùa special-use forest, on the border between Sơn La and the Trạm Tấu area. The most used trail starts from Xím Vàng: about 8km, roughly 4 hours on foot to the hut, and groups usually do it in 2 days 1 night.

**Quick summary:**

- **Height:** Sa Mu Peak is 2.756m. Some photo posts give other figures; we use 2.756m, the figure that Tuổi Trẻ, Nhân Dân and Viet Nam News all report.
- **Two ways up:** from the foot of the mountain in Xím Vàng (the most popular) and from Chống Tra village in the former Háng Đồng area, now part of Tà Xùa commune.
- **Walking distance:** the Xím Vàng route is about 8km, about 4 hours to the hut. One article gives a total of about 13km from the foot of the mountain to the summit.
- **Hut:** built by local people, it takes about 40 guests and has blankets, pillows, water and meals; you must contact them in advance.
- **Guide:** strongly needed. The trail is new, has no signposts and it is very easy to get lost.
- **Season:** clouds are best from December to February, rhododendrons bloom around February to April, and maple leaves turn color from September to November.

## What mountain is Sa Mu, or U Bò, and where is it compared with Tà Xùa?

Sa Mu is one of the three big peaks of the Tà Xùa special-use forest, on the same range as Tà Xùa Peak. The summit lies on the border between former Bắc Yên (Sơn La) and Trạm Tấu (former Yên Bái, now part of Lào Cai province). "U Bò" is another name that groups use for the same mountain.

Compared with the Tà Xùa tourist area, the nearest foothill is Xím Vàng commune to the northwest:

- From central Tà Xùa to Xím Vàng along provincial road 112 via Đỉnh Gió is about 15 to 20km, around 40 minutes by motorbike.
- From Bắc Yên town to the Xím Vàng side of the mountain is about 28 to 32km, depending on the starting point.
- The second route starts from Chống Tra village, near the Dinosaur Spine, 12 to 15km from central Tà Xùa.

Because Xím Vàng has few places for visitors to sleep, groups usually stay at a Tà Xùa homestay the night before and the night after. That is why the Sa Mu route is often called "Tà Xùa trekking" even though the summit is not Tà Xùa Peak.

## What is Hang Chú, and is there a Hang Chú trek?

Many people search "Hang Chú trekking" when looking for trails around Tà Xùa. Hang Chú is the name of **a former commune** behind Xím Vàng, not a cave or a peak. Since 01/07/2025, under Resolution 1681/NQ-UBTVQH15, Hang Chú commune and Xím Vàng commune merged into the new Xím Vàng commune.

The Hang Chú area comes up for two reasons:

- **Khe Hổ ancient rock field:** 9 large granite boulders scattered across the valley floor, with many carvings on their surfaces.
- **One way to the foot of Sa Mu:** some guides list Hang Chú together with Xím Vàng and Chống Tra as starting points.

The walking route that groups describe clearly, with its trail, hut and guides, is Sa Mu from Xím Vàng, so this article focuses on that route. In Khe Cải village, Tà Xùa also has [Hang Chú restaurant](/nha-hang/nha-hang-hang-chu-ta-xua), which serves hotpot and grill trays for groups; it only shares a name with the former commune.

## How does the Sa Mu route from Xím Vàng go, and how long does it take?

Based on accounts from groups who have done it and from news articles, the Xím Vàng route goes like this:

1. **Reach the foot of the mountain:** ride from Tà Xùa or Bắc Yên to Xím Vàng. The guide sets a meeting point in the village.
2. **Cross the forest to the hut:** about 8km, roughly 4 hours on foot. The path passes hill fields and then enters primeval forest, with many old trees covered in moss and ferns.
3. **Overnight at the hut:** built by local people, it takes about 40 guests and has blankets, pillows, drinking water and meals.
4. **Summit:** get up early, cross grassy meadows and streams above 2.000m, pass dense rhododendrons near 2.700m, then reach the 2.756m marker.
5. **Descend:** return to the hut to eat, then go down to the foot of the mountain the same day.

The Chống Tra route is a little different. Young men from the village take visitors by motorbike along a single track to the forest edge. After about 5km through the forest the group stops for lunch, and the section near the summit climbs gradually from about 2.600m. Some fit groups do it as a day trip, but the comfortable schedule is 2 days 1 night.

Difficulty is moderate. One article says the route is not too hard and suits beginners to trekking; another person rated it about 6/10. What makes it hard is the weather: rain makes moss and soil slippery, and thick fog makes you lose your bearings.

## Do you need a guide, and where can you hire one?

Yes. All the guides stress one point: the trail is new, has no signposts, and a moment of inattention can take you into another part of the forest. Do not go alone, and do not try to find your own way with a map.

At present, Xím Vàng locals organize guiding, carry gear, cook and arrange the hut. On the Chống Tra route, local Hmong people who know the terrain take on guiding. Two ways to find one:

- Ask your Tà Xùa homestay owner to introduce a guide they know.
- Contact the hut owner or a guide team in Xím Vàng directly a few days ahead, since the hut has limited space.

Before paying a deposit, ask everything: the meeting point, start time, whether the price includes meals and bedding at the hut, how many kg the guide will carry for you, and how dates change in bad weather. A detailed list of questions is in the Tà Xùa Peak trekking article above, and it applies to Sa Mu too.

## Which season is best for Sa Mu, and what should you bring?

Each season on Sa Mu has something of its own:

- **December to February:** good chances of a sea of clouds at sunrise and sunset. This is also the coldest time.
- **Around February to April:** rhododendrons bloom on the upper part of the route.
- **September to November:** maple leaves in the forest turn yellow and red.
- **Mid-year rainy season:** forest trails are slippery; better not to go.

Central Tà Xùa sits at about 1.500m, and winter nights are usually 5 to 10°C. The hut and the summit are above 2.000m, so they are colder. Minimum gear:

- Hiking shoes with grippy soles that you have broken in, plus spare socks.
- Three layers: a thermal layer, fleece or wool, and a windproof jacket. A wool hat and gloves.
- A rain suit and waterproof bags for your phone and clothes.
- A headlamp and a power bank.
- Energy food: chocolate and dried fruit; drinking water for the climb to the hut.
- Insect repellent, long clothes, personal medicine and cash. The nearest ATM is in Bắc Yên.

How to dress warm and the temperature for each month are in [How cold is Tà Xùa in winter](/kinh-nghiem/ta-xua-mua-dong-lanh-mac-gi). For the cloud season by month, see [When is the best time to visit Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/ta-xua-mua-nao-dep-nhat).

## How do you get to Sa Mu from Hanoi, and where do you stay?

A tight schedule for working people: take the cabin bus Thursday night, arrive in Tà Xùa Friday morning and rest, trek on Saturday and Sunday, sleep in Tà Xùa again on Sunday, hunt clouds on Monday morning, then head down at noon. At minimum you need 1 night at a homestay before and 1 night after the climb.

**Buses from Hanoi.** Five cabin bus companies run every night, leaving Hanoi at 21h to 22h and reaching central Tà Xùa around 6h. Round-trip tickets per person: [Khánh Châu](/xe-cabin/nha-xe-khanh-chau) 500.000đ, Xuân Trường 550.000đ, Thành Mỹ 600.000đ, Tiến Thành 600.000đ, Quang Nghĩa 650.000đ. To compare the sleeper cabins, read [comparing bus companies to Tà Xùa](/kinh-nghiem/so-sanh-nha-xe-di-ta-xua).

**From Tà Xùa to Xím Vàng.** Ride a rented motorbike from the center (Honda Wave 180.000đ, Yamaha PG1 280.000đ per day) or ask your guide to pick you up. Some sections are tough; if you are not a confident rider, take a [local motorbike taxi](/xe-om) and agree on the fare beforehand.

- [La Chếu Homestay](/homestay/la-cheu-homestay): Đỉnh Gió area, on the way to Xím Vàng, 19 of 20 rooms have heated mattresses, priced 675.000đ to 1.665.000đ.

- [Tà Xùa Hill](/homestay/ta-xua-hill): in the center, all 21 rooms have heated mattresses, priced 375.000đ to 1.150.000đ.

## Frequently asked questions

### How high is Sa Mu Peak?

Most sources say Sa Mu Peak, also called U Bò, is 2.756m high. It lies in the Tà Xùa special-use forest, on the border between Sơn La and the Trạm Tấu area.

### Where does the Sa Mu trek start?

The most popular trail starts at the foot of the mountain in Xím Vàng commune, 15 to 20km from central Tà Xùa. The second trail starts from Chống Tra village, near the Dinosaur Spine.

### How many days does the Sa Mu trek take?

Groups usually do 2 days 1 night, sleeping at the hut. The Xím Vàng route takes about 4 hours on foot for the 8km to the hut. Some groups take 3 days 2 nights to go at a relaxed pace.

### Can beginners climb Sa Mu?

Yes, if you are fit enough to walk all day and go with a local guide. Difficulty is moderate; rain and thick fog make the route much harder.

### What is Hang Chú in Tà Xùa?

Hang Chú is a former commune behind Xím Vàng that merged into Xím Vàng commune on 01/07/2025. The area has the Khe Hổ ancient rock field with 9 carved granite boulders, and it is one way to the foot of Sa Mu.

### Is Sa Mu the same as Tà Xùa Peak?

No. Sa Mu is 2.756m, and Tà Xùa Peak is 2.865m. Both lie in the Tà Xùa special-use forest, but they are two different climbing routes.
